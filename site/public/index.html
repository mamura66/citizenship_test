Countries

One account, whichever test.

You choose your country when you sign up, and it stays with your account. That's deliberate: every answer we record belongs to a section of one country's test, so moving between them mid-preparation would leave you with a progress report about a test you aren't taking. If you pick the wrong one, tell us and we'll fix it.

Which country comes next isn't really up to us. A country can only be here if its government publishes the actual questions — the United States does, and so does Germany, where the full Einbürgerungstest catalogue is available to anyone. Where that's true, adding a country is a set of questions and a translation, and nothing else changes.

Where it isn't true, we'd rather say so than keep you waiting. Canada and Australia publish a study guide but not the questions themselves, and the Life in the UK question bank is confidential Crown copyright — it isn't published and it isn't going to be. We won't invent questions and call them official, so for those tests the honest answer is that we don't have one yet.

If your country publishes its questions and isn't here, tell us — that's genuinely how this list grows.

Questions

Straight answers.

Is this a government service?

No. It's an independent study aid, not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any government or immigration authority. The official questions are public government material; everything around them is ours.

Can you advise me on my application?

No, and we won't pretend otherwise. This teaches the civics test. It doesn't advise on your case, prepare or submit any form, or take part in your interview. For that, speak to an immigration lawyer or an accredited representative.

Which countries can I use this for?

The United States today, and Germany's Einbürgerungstest next. A country needs its government to publish the real question pool, which not every country does — Canada and Australia publish a study guide but not the questions, and the Life in the UK question bank is confidential and unpublished. Where the questions are public, the whole app follows: the questions, the pass mark, and the interface in that country's own language.

Which version of the test applies to me?

For the United States, that depends on your Form N-400 filing date and the rules in effect for your application. Both live versions are included, and you can switch between them whenever you like — check which one applies to you with the authority handling your case.

Do the answers stay up to date?

Yes. A few questions ask who currently holds an office, and those change with elections and appointments. Each one is checked against the government's own records before every release.

Do I need an account?

Yes, including for the free part. It's how your progress follows you between devices, and how the one free practice test stays yours rather than resetting every time you clear your browser. We hold your email address, your progress, and whether you've paid. Nothing else.

Does the microphone listen to me?

No. Interview practice only speaks, using your browser or phone's built-in text-to-speech. The microphone is never accessed and no audio is recorded or sent anywhere.

Is the $9.99 a subscription?

No. It's a single payment, plus whatever tax your country charges. Nothing renews, and there's nothing to cancel. It's tied to your account, so signing in on another device brings it with you.

Do I need the app?

No. Everything runs in your browser, on a phone or a computer. An iPhone app is on the way, and Android after it. A purchase in the app and a purchase here are separate, because Apple handles its own payments — so buy in the place you plan to study.
